# Apps

`offsider app info|launch|stop|open` acts on one installed app and nothing else on the device. Name it with `--app <bundle id or package>`; without one, Offsider takes `offsider.login.json`'s app, then a physical device's only pinned app (exit 64 with neither).

- `offsider app info --app <id> --device <DEVICE_ID> --json` reads the version (`appVersion`) and `build`, and on Android the `installer` (`com.android.vending` is Google Play, null for adb), `firstInstalled`, `lastUpdated`, `debuggable` and, from Android 13, `notifications` (`granted` or `denied`). An iPhone app installed from Xcode reads installer `developer`. Use it to prove the build under test is the one installed. Its JSON `untrusted` list names the app's own text.
- `offsider app launch --app <id>` starts the app as a tap on its icon does; `--restart` stops it first, for a cold start. JSON `start` is `cold` (a new process) or `warm` (it was running); Android adds `milliseconds`, its launch time.
- `offsider app stop --app <id>` ends the app's process; `wasRunning` says whether there was one. On Android this is Force stop, which also clears the app's notifications and alarms.
- `offsider app open '<link>' --app <id>` sends a deep link or universal link to that app alone, never a browser or chooser; quote the link so `&` and `?` survive your shell. `--restart` opens it cold. A link the app has no screen for exits 1 `link_not_handled`. On a simulator iOS picks the app that claims the link, Offsider answers the `Open in` prompt only when it names that app, and checks the app is in front; on an iPhone or iPad the app receives it as an open-URL call, not as a tapped universal link. `intent:`, `file:`, `content:`, `javascript:` and `data:` links are refused (exit 64).
- `launch`, `stop` and `open` lock the device like input; `info` only reads. After `launch` or `open`, wait for the screen you expect (`wait --id <id>`) before acting on it.
- On a physical device the app must be one its allowance permits: browsers, home screens, system apps and apps managed policy denies exit 1 `app_not_allowed` with nothing sent, also on a phone allowed for any app. A locked iPhone exits 7 `device_locked`: ask the user to unlock it.
